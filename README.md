## 🎯 Research Focus

I study how extreme conditions—from spaceflight to disease—reveal fundamental biology, and how AI can accelerate these discoveries. As Director of Spaceflight Research at Weill Cornell Medicine, I've led molecular profiling for **Inspiration4**, **Polaris Dawn**, **Fram2**, and **Axiom-2** missions, contributing to ~40 papers in the Space Omics and Medical Atlas (SOMA).

**Current interests:**
- Foundation models for perturbation biology (scGPT, Geneformer)
- Evaluation frameworks for AI in scientific reasoning
- Multi-omics integration under novel conditions

## 📊 Key Insight from Benchmarking

> **LLMs reason about biology better than they recall domain-specific facts** (86% vs 51%), but this gap closes with RAG. More importantly, Claude shows only 3% hallucination rate, preferring honest uncertainty—exactly what scientific AI needs.

This finding directly informed my understanding of how to deploy AI in research workflows.

<details>
<summary><h2>💡 Philosophy</h2></summary>

> Current AI operates like a *highly capable but amnesic postdoc*—brilliant in the moment but unable to connect insights across experimental iterations. Language models learn from text that *describes* biology; they don't learn biology *itself*.

**Closing this gap requires:**

- 🧫 **Perturbation atlases** with systematic interventions
- 🏗️ **Infrastructure** for AI-ready biological data
- 🔄 **Integration** with automated wet labs for closed-loop learning
- 📏 **Rigorous evaluation** measuring genuine scientific utility
